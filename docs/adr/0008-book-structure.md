# Book structure: every chapter is a piece, climbing in structural complexity

Each chapter of the Sound Garden book is a complete piece of music, and the chapters climb from simple structures to complex ones, in DSP mathematics and in musical form at the same time. Side explorations branch off a chapter and can be followed on their own. We chose this over Puckette's topic-by-topic order, and over following the author's biography, because the book has three goals at once: the book itself, growing the author's skill in composition, sound design, performance, maths and DSP together, and producing finished music. A chapter that ends in a piece serves all three, and climbing in structure gives the concepts the steady build of a textbook.

A piece is a single program whose form unfolds from its own structure, not a recorded performance of edits: the book renders it deterministically and its listing is the complete score, in keeping with code as the libretto of its sound. Live performance as a subject, and replaying a performance session, are deferred until the ladder reaches them.

Programs are presented as linear text, because Sound Garden programs are linear in the end; the spatial editor is linked for free exploration and used for some larger pieces whose layout is part of their aesthetics. The book teaches the theory behind each op deeply enough to rebuild it, but not the op implementations themselves.

Mathematics is in the main text, not boxed away. The author is the model reader: someone who wants to grow their mathematical understanding through the book, so maths is introduced when the music needs it and built up from there rather than assumed. The voice is "we": author and reader exploring together.
